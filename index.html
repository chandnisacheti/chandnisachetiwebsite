My story

I grew up with a women’s health condition no doctor could diagnose.

I tried over 11 therapies. What changed my life seven years later was going after the root cause and building my own treatment stack.

That experience is what drives me.

It’s carried me from Hong Kong to a B.S. in Cognitive Science & Entrepreneurship at UCLA, and now to an M.S. in Biomedical Engineering, specializing in neural engineering, at Columbia University.

Along the way, I’ve come to see Eastern and Western medicine not as opposing systems but as two lenses on the same person. Now I’m building the bridge: root-cause care that reads each person’s body and brain, and uses AI to tailor treatment to them.

Projects

Closed-loop insulin

I built a glucometer from a test strip, an op-amp circuit and an Arduino, then a closed-loop PID controller (P 0.04, I 0.0009, D 0.1) that doses insulin on its own, holding a diabetic model in the healthy range through a meal.

Peak glucose 205 → 113 mg/dL

ECG classification

A CNN trained on 27,765 raw ECGs to spot heart attacks, conduction problems, ST changes and hypertrophy, then tuned for the most accuracy per minute of training.

83% accuracy in 3.3 min of training

Precision thermometer

I built a thermistor thermometer on a Wheatstone bridge with Steinhart-Hart calibration, then hunted down its error in a real water bath. Averaging tamed the noise, but only precision resistors fixed the offset.

Worst-case error 14.9 → 1.45 °C
