How I got into neurotech

Under the door

I first discovered my fascination with brain-computer interfaces back in high school. That curiosity led me to study Cognitive Science at UCLA.

But very quickly, I realized it was a lot more challenging than I expected. Conceptually, I was captivated. Technically, I deeply struggled. I didn’t think I was capable enough to thrive in the industry, so I convinced myself I wasn’t cut out for neurotech.

Two years later, I decided to give it one final shot. I had no neurotech experience. My first step was using the resources available at UCLA, so my first goal: get into a neurotech lab. I reached out again and again. No one wrote back.

So I stopped waiting for a reply. I printed my resume, tracked down the lab’s address in a hidden corner of the internet, and slid it under the door.

Two days later, my phone rang. My resume had turned up on a table inside their locked lab. (The janitor, I later learned, had put it there.) They were so flabbergasted they thought I’d broken in. They had to call me.

That call turned into an interview. The interview turned into a spot in the Kuhn Lab, doing hands-on research in transcranial focused ultrasound.

From there, I kept going. I joined CruX, UCLA’s neurotechnology club, and connected with every neurotech founder I could, at conferences and over coffee chats. That led me to Wave Neuroscience, working across product strategy and data science.

I still often felt like the least experienced person in the room: daunted and full of questions. But with that came immense space to grow, and I’d never felt more driven to learn.

It started with a resume under a door. I’ve been finding ways in ever since.
