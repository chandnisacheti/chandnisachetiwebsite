Somewhere between uncomfortable and transformative.

A rule I live by

The 50/50 rule

I started 2026 with one resolution: the 50/50 Rule.

The rule is simple. Every day, do something with a 50% chance of succeeding. Or failing. Small or big. The goal was to expand my way of living.

This year that’s looked like joining an intermediate salsa class and being easily the worst in the room. Pitching Refuel Movement at Bruin Tank 2026 and Shark Tank ABC’s Casting Call. Showing up at event after event to pitch the Lives Without Knives Fest partnership model, hearing no more times than I can count. Walking into fitness studios cold to pitch Refuel Movement.

It also looked like applying to graduate programs without knowing where I’d land. Becoming a NeuroGum brand ambassador while launching NEUROHACK. Modeling at the Asian Hall of Fame surrounded by a world I’d never been part of before. And so many more moments that lived somewhere between uncomfortable and transformative.

Now, nearly halfway through the year, I’ve been reflecting on how it’s actually going. Definitely a lot more uncomfortable than I anticipated, but I guess that’s where the growth happens.

The more personally tied you are to something, the more a rejection cuts. But I’ve always believed in the law of large numbers: the more I put myself out there, the more opens up.

And when things land? They open doors you didn’t even know existed. Getting into Columbia University was one of those moments.

The past six months have been deeply rooted in Los Angeles. But as I move to New York soon, I’m incredibly excited to see how the 50/50 rule shifts: new city, new rooms, new chances to be the most out-of-place person there.

This isn’t just a resolution anymore, it’s becoming the philosophy I want to live my life by.

Can’t wait to see what the full pile looks like by the end of 2026.

Rooms I had no business being in

Finding the third door

When I came across Biohackers World in Los Angeles, tickets were $300. I couldn’t afford that, so I went looking for my third door. The Third Door is one of my favorite books: there’s always a way in, even when it looks like there isn’t.

Their website listed spots for volunteers and media partners. I’d gotten into plenty of events by volunteering, but this time I wanted to really be part of the conference. So I signed Lives Without Knives up as a media partner. I’d never interviewed big exhibitors and companies before, and neither had LWK. We’d only ever worked with local plant-based businesses. I applied anyway, and somehow I got in.

I spent those two days covering every single vendor booth and talking to everyone. By the end of the conference, people knew me and wanted to talk to me. I left with an incredible network and a crash course in the biohacking and biotech space.
