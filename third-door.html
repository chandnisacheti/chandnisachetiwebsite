Rooms I found my way into

Finding the third door

When I came across Biohackers World in Los Angeles, tickets were $300. I couldn’t afford that, so I went looking for my third door. The Third Door is one of my favorite books: there’s always a way in, even when it looks like there isn’t.

Their website listed spots for volunteers and media partners. I’d gotten into plenty of events by volunteering, but this time I wanted to really be part of the conference. So I signed Lives Without Knives up as a media partner. I’d never interviewed big exhibitors and companies before, and neither had LWK. We’d only ever worked with local plant-based businesses. I applied anyway, and somehow I got in.

I spent those two days covering every single vendor booth and talking to everyone, interviewing health tech companies and founders live for our Instagram.

In between, I sat in on panels across health tech, neurotechnology and AI-driven wellness. Kim Ressler of SNiP Nutrigenomics talked about DNA-personalized nutrition built on 103+ actionable genes. As she put it, “Slowing down doesn’t have to be a side effect of life.” On the floor, I saw cellular insights from Jinfiniti Precision Medicine, neuro-focused tools from BrainTap and clinical innovations from weber medical.

One question stayed with me the whole time: in a society where being unhealthy has become normalized, how can we re-establish optimal health as the baseline? Everyone in that room was working on an answer. We’re no longer just treating chronic illness. We’re building systems for prevention, personalization and optimization.

By the end of the conference, people knew me and wanted to talk to me. I’d connected with people like Dr. Christina Rahm and Jin-Xiong She, PhD, who are blending holistic wisdom with future-forward tools. I left with an incredible network and a crash course in the biohacking and biotech space.

I couldn’t afford a $300 ticket. I walked out knowing exactly which space I wanted to build in.
